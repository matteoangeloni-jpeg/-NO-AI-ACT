Press kit

Official materials for covering NO AI ACT: descriptions, factsheet, images, credits and references. Public version v5.3.0. Kit updated 22 November 2026. Kit text and images are reusable under CC BY 4.0.

01Short description (~40 words)

NO AI ACT is a free, open-source serious game about the EU AI Act: in the browser, with no account, players investigate 14 AI systems in a fictional unregulated city, examine evidence and classify risk. Available in Italian, English, French, German, Spanish and European Portuguese.

02Medium description (~100 words)

Local saves and optional aggregate statistics. Only with adult consent: a session record for 90 days; guided-path text requires separate consent. Withdraw, erase or export from the game bar.

03Extended description

NO AI ACT starts from a question: how do you teach a complex regulation like the AI Act to people who are not lawyers? The project's answer is to flip the perspective — show a society where those rules do not exist. In the game's fictional city, AI systems operate unconstrained: social scores deciding access to services, unsupervised hiring algorithms, unlabelled deepfakes, opaque medical triage.

Playing as a civic inspector, you reconstruct each case from the evidence: documents, testimony, technical logs. You decide the risk classification under the AI Act's categories, identify who is responsible (provider or deployer), choose proportionate corrective measures and file a report. A debrief explains why each decision holds or fails, and the city's indicators — trust, rights, innovation — respond over time. The game deliberately simplifies the law for educational purposes and treats ambiguous scenarios as contestable: it is not legal advice, and its educational effectiveness has not yet been empirically validated. Dedicated guides, activities and lesson plans for teachers and trainers are available on this site.

06Suggested editorial angles

  • Teaching law through play: what happens when the AI Act is learned by inspecting an unregulated city rather than reading a textbook.
  • Digital civic education: a free tool with no account for discussing AI evidence, rights and responsibilities in class.
  • Open source and education: a fully open individual project whose privacy promises are enforced by automated tests.
  • Regulation in reverse: telling the story of the AI Act through its absence — the counterfactual world of 2032.
  • Bureaucracy as game design: case files, evidence, signature and stamp turn administrative reasoning into play.
  • Procedural visual system: institutions, documents, diagrams and city state are generated by the Phaser engine rather than relying on static illustrations.

07Short author biography

Matteo Angeloni is a PhD candidate at the University of Tuscia, researching artificial intelligence's impact on education, decision-making, reskilling and change governance. He is the creator, author and developer of NO AI ACT, responsible for its game design, software, educational content and internal editorial review. The project is independent and does not provide legal advice.

10Privacy statement (reusable)

NO AI ACT requires no account. Answers, classifications and saves stay on the device. The game automatically sends aggregate usage counters without names, emails or player identifiers. Sending can be disabled in the game bar; Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control are respected. Public pages also use cookie-free Cloudflare Web Analytics. The infrastructure processes connection metadata needed to deliver and protect the service.

11Version 5.0 and teaching resources

Four modes use the same 14 cases: Duty Shift offers a profile-based path; Free Investigation lets players choose from the map; Surprise Inspection changes the case order; Review revisits poorly closed cases and is available only when there are some. The selected duration is a planning budget, not a countdown.

Teacher mode and downloadable materials support facilitated discussion, not a classroom dashboard. Guides, glossaries, activities, lesson plans, workshops, press kits and the methodology dossier are available in Italian, English, French, German, Spanish and European Portuguese. Each language covers all 37 website sections and all 14 game cases.

Method and limits

Educational simulation — not legal advice

NO AI ACT is an educational tool. It deliberately simplifies Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (AI Act) and does not replace the legal text or professional advice.
